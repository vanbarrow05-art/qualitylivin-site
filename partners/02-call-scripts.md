# 2. Call, voicemail, email and text scripts

Current complete caller handoff: [08-complete-sales-playbook.txt](08-complete-sales-playbook.txt). Use its current offer and commission wording for live calls; earlier provider examples below are reference templates and must be checked against the actual offer.

Say it in your own words. The structure matters more than the exact lines. For a provider-recruitment call, the goal is an agreed next step for the three-free-lead pilot; record referral acceptance and permission to publish separately. For a business that needs cleaning, use **Script I**: qualify the location, collect the manager's available time slots for an in-person estimate, and submit the existing cleaning form to the Quality Living janitorial pipeline. The provider marketing packages do not price the customer's cleaning job.

> Fill-ins: **[Name]** = you · **[Owner]** = their decision-maker · **[Company]** · **[City]** · **[Rating]** e.g. "4.9 with 180 Google reviews" (only say what you've actually seen)

## A. Local / regional owner-operator (the main call)

**Opener (first 15 seconds)**
> "Hi, is this [Owner]? This is [Name] with Quality Living. We run a local services directory and a home-and-lifestyle magazine here in [City]. I'm calling because you're one of the top-rated cleaning companies in the area: I saw your [Rating]. Do you have 60 seconds? I'll tell you why I called and you can tell me if it's a fit."

**Reason for the call**
> "We get people in [City] asking for cleaning estimates: homes, short-term rentals, offices. We only send them to a short list of top-rated companies. I'd like to send you **three leads free**. No card, no contract, no fee on those three. If they're good, we can talk about keeping them coming. If they're not, you've lost nothing."

**Qualify (ask, don't pitch)**
1. "What kind of jobs do you want more of: residential, recurring, move-outs, commercial?"
2. "Which areas or ZIP codes do you cover?"
3. "How many new jobs could you take on in a typical month right now?"
4. "When a lead comes in, who calls them back, and how fast?"
5. "Do you use online booking or a calendar link?" *(Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Calendly, Google Calendar, or GHL)*
6. "Are you licensed and insured? Could you email a certificate of insurance?"

**The ask (consent to list)**
> "Great. Here's what happens next: I'll email a one-page partner agreement, about two minutes to read. It covers the three free leads, how leads are delivered, and it gives us your OK to show [Company] on our network map with your services and your [Google] rating linked to the source. Once you reply 'agreed', you're live and the first lead goes out as soon as we have one in your area. What's the best email?"

**Close the call**
> "Last thing: what's the best number for leads to go to, and is text OK for lead alerts?" *(Get explicit permission for texts.)*
> "Perfect. You'll get the email in the next hour. Thanks, [Owner]."

## B. National brands & franchise systems (Two Maids, Molly Maid, Jan-Pro, etc.)

Corporate won't take leads directly: **franchisees** do the work. Your goal at corporate is **permission to approach franchisees** or a **national referral partnership**. Ask for **franchise development, marketing, or partnerships**, not sales.

**To the operator / main line**
> "Hi, I'm [Name] with Quality Living, a local services marketplace and lifestyle magazine. I'm looking for whoever handles marketing partnerships or national accounts. Could you point me to the right person or email?"

**To the partnerships / marketing contact**
> "We generate cleaning estimate requests from homeowners, short-term rental hosts and offices, and we route them to top-rated local providers. Several of your locations already meet our quality bar. We'd like to offer your franchisees three free leads each in the markets we cover, with no fee, and then a referral or marketing option they can choose individually. Is there a preferred-vendor or local-marketing program we should go through, or are franchisees free to work with lead partners directly?"

**If they say franchisees decide:** "Great. Is there a franchisee directory or regional manager you'd suggest I start with, so I approach them the way you'd like?"
**If there's a vendor program:** ask for requirements, the application link, insurance/COI needs, and the timeline. Log it in the CRM as `national-vendor-application`.

**Then call franchise owners using Script A**, and open with: "I'm calling the [Brand] location in [City] specifically, because your location's reviews stand out."

## C. Gatekeeper

> "Hi, this is [Name] with Quality Living. I'm calling for [Owner] about sending [Company] some free customer leads. Is now a good time, or when's the best time to catch them?"

If they ask "is this a sales call?":
> "It's an offer of free leads: three customer inquiries at no cost. I just need two minutes with whoever decides which jobs you take."

## D. Voicemail (under 25 seconds)

> "Hi [Owner], [Name] with Quality Living here. We have people in [City] requesting cleaning estimates, and you're one of the top-rated companies we'd like to send them to. First three leads are free, no contract. Call or text me back at [your number]. Again, [Name], Quality Living, [your number]. Thanks."

Leave a maximum of **2 voicemails**, 3 business days apart, then switch to email.

## F. Text messages (only after they've agreed to texts)

- **Lead alert:** "Quality Living lead for [Company]: [first name], [service], [ZIP], wants [timing]. Details in your email. Please call within 1 hour. Reply STOP to opt out."
- **Check-in after a lead:** "Hi [Owner], did you connect with [first name] about the [service]? Reply 1 = booked, 2 = quoted, 3 = no answer, 4 = not a fit."
- **After lead 3:** "That was your 3rd free lead. Want to keep them coming? Reply YES and I'll call you with the options (referral-only or a monthly plan)."

Don't send cold texts to people who haven't opted in. See the compliance notes in `04-pipeline-and-training.md`.

## G. The upgrade call (after lead 3, or about 2 weeks in)

> "Hi [Owner], quick check-in on the three leads. How did they go? *(Listen. Write down booked/closed and job value.)*
> Here's what most partners do next. Option one: stay a **Referral Partner**. We keep sending leads and take 10% of what you collect from them, nothing upfront. Option two: if you'd rather we bring you *more* work, our **AI Autopilot & Growth** plan at $800 a month runs your booking, follow-up and an AI receptionist so no lead slips, and posts for you three to five times a week. Option three, for multiple areas: **Scale & Media** at $1,600, which adds video content and a feature in our magazine. Which sounds closest to what you need right now?"

**If they say no to everything:** "Understood. There is no automatic referral charge. We would need your agreement to a referral arrangement before sending chargeable leads."

## H. Objection handling

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Is this a scam / what's the catch?" | "Fair question. The first three leads are free so you can judge the quality before spending anything. No card, nothing to sign except permission to send leads and list you." |
| "We're too busy." | "That's why we picked you. Would you rather get leads only in certain ZIP codes, or only for higher-value jobs like recurring or commercial? We can filter." |
| "We tried Angi / Thumbtack and the leads were junk." | "Those platforms sell the same lead to several companies. We send each lead to one provider, and invalid leads don't count. And with us you pay on collected revenue, not per lead." |
| "What is your commission?" | "The first three eligible introductions have no referral fee. After that, a referral-only arrangement uses a fixed rate agreed in writing, normally 10–20% of eligible collected revenue, with the covered work and duration defined first. Declining a package does not start commission billing." |
| "The commission is too much." | "Let's compare the agreed services and your margins. We can discuss a different scope or a monthly package; you choose the terms before any chargeable referrals." |
| "Send me an email." | "Happy to. So I send the right thing: is it residential, commercial, or both you want more of? And which areas?" *(Then send email E.)* |
| "We already do our own marketing." | "Great, then this is just extra demand on top. If you want help later with booking, follow-up or posts, we can talk then." |
| "How do you track the 10%?" | "You report booked jobs monthly. Or, easier, we connect to your booking calendar so it's automatic. It's all in the one-page agreement." |
| "Can I see the leads first?" | "That's exactly what the three free ones are for." |

## I. Commercial cleaning customer — in-person estimate request

**Before calling:** check the prospect's official website for the right location and publicly listed business/manager information, then confirm uncertain details on the call. Open the existing cleaning form: https://api.leadconnectorhq.com/widget/form/2CZsk7kdEPJcdbYk33Tf. The owner confirmed that submissions route to the existing Quality Living janitorial pipeline in GoHighLevel. The caller collects the customer's available time slots and submits the form; the janitorial team assigns the representative and confirms the visit. A separate caller booking calendar is not required.

**Opening**
> "Hi, I'm [Name] with Quality Living. Who handles cleaning arrangements for your [confirmed location] location? We're checking whether an in-person cleaning estimate would be useful for your business."

**Ask in this order**
1. **Location:** "Which location needs cleaning? Could I confirm the street address, suite, city, state and ZIP?"
2. **In-house cleaners:** "Do you currently have in-house cleaners? Do you also use an outside cleaning company?"
3. **Legal business name:** "What legal business name should we put on the estimate? Is the name on the building different?" Record the legal name and trading name separately; flag an unknown name for follow-up.
4. **Managers:** "Who manages cleaning at that location? Could I have their full name, role and best work contact details? Is another manager involved in approving the work, and who would meet us on-site?"
5. **Scope:** "What would you like help with, roughly how large is the space, and how often is cleaning needed?"
6. **Available time slots:** "What two dates and time slots would work for [manager] to meet someone at the property for an in-person estimate? What timezone is the property in?" Read back each date, start/end time and timezone. These are the customer's available windows, not a confirmed team booking.

**If they have in-house cleaners**
> "Understood. Is there any work your team would like help with, such as deep cleaning, floors or extra coverage?"

**Close and submit**
> "I'll submit your details and those time slots to our janitorial team. They'll contact [manager] to confirm who is coming and the exact time for the in-person estimate. What's the best work phone or email for that confirmation?"

Use the customer's actual contact details in the form, not the caller's. Put the new business/manager/time-slot details in **Additional Details or Comments** until dedicated fields exist. Submit once, verify the form acknowledgement and record the submission time. If it fails, record the failure and follow up; do not say the request was submitted. The team handles the existing pipeline assignment and appointment confirmation.

**After the form acknowledges submission**
> "Your request has been submitted. Our janitorial team will follow up to confirm the in-person estimate. The visit time is not confirmed yet."

**Commission rebuttal — cleaning customer**
> "Quality Living may be paid by the cleaning provider through a marketing agreement or an agreed referral fee. The provider supplies your cleaning quote. Our team will explain any charge to you for the estimate or the work before you agree."

If they ask the exact percentage:
> "Our referral-only arrangements can be 10% to 20% of eligible revenue the provider collects, depending on the agreed service. I'll have the team confirm the terms for the provider handling your request."

Do not claim every provider pays a commission, that the visit is free, that the customer will never pay a fee, or that commission cannot affect a quote without the actual terms supporting it.

**Commission rebuttal — provider being recruited**
> "The first three eligible introductory leads carry no lead or referral fee. If you choose referral-only after that, we agree a fixed rate, normally 10% to 20% of eligible revenue actually collected from referred work, in writing before chargeable referrals. We also agree what work and time period it covers. There is no automatic commission just because you decline a monthly package."

**Other quick responses**
- "We already have a cleaner." → "Are you satisfied with the coverage, or would a comparison or help with occasional work be useful?"
- "Just give me a price." → "The provider needs to see the space and confirm the scope to prepare the cleaning estimate. What times could your manager meet on-site?"
- "The manager isn't here." → "Who should I speak with, and when is a good time to reach them? I can submit a request for the team to follow up; I won't mark a visit confirmed."
- "Can you guarantee Tuesday at 10?" → "I'll include Tuesday at 10, with your timezone, as your preferred slot. The janitorial team will confirm whether they can attend."
- "Not interested / don't call." → "Understood. Thank you." Record the opt-out and end the pitch.

**Record:** website/source checked, exact service location, in-house/outside cleaning, legal name/DBA, manager and attendee details, scope, two available time slots and timezone, preferred confirmation contact, form acknowledgement/submission time and next action. Preserve separate requests for separate locations. Full field and submission instructions: [07-cleaning-walkthrough-intake.md](07-cleaning-walkthrough-intake.md).
